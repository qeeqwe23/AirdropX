Evidence from V50 v2.0.4 residual/estimator audit (uploaded 2026-08-17)

This file documents why v2.0.5 is a zero-persistence estimator A/B test rather
than an arbitrary retune.

cfg2 5-second windows, persistent input-disturbance state norm (d1,d2):
  105-110 s  p95 ~= 0.0103
  110-115 s  p95 ~= 0.0146
  115-120 s  p95 ~= 0.0225
  120-125 s  p95 ~= 0.0352
  125-130 s  p95 ~= 0.0571
  130-135 s  p95 ~= 0.0973
  135-140 s  p95 ~= 0.1835

The MPC MV deviations are almost the opposite of the estimated load states:
  120-125 s: median d1=+0.02194, elevator deviation=-0.02372
             median d2=-0.01861, throttle deviation=+0.02100
             correlations corr(dElev,-d1) ~= 1.0000,
                          corr(dThrottle,-d2) ~= 1.0000
  130-135 s: median d1=+0.06420, elevator deviation=-0.07119
             median d2=-0.04111, throttle deviation=+0.05187
  140-145 s: median d1=+0.25617, elevator deviation=-0.28552
             median d2=-0.12856, throttle deviation=+0.15480

The equivalent-load residual and estimator also become highly correlated after
115 s.  This supports a causal test of whether persistent integration is a
principal positive-feedback amplifier.  It does NOT by itself prove that the
input-disturbance integrators are the original source of the model residual.
